# Channel specifications

Each asset has its own length, structure, and rhythm rules. These are non-negotiable formatting standards — voice and argument flow across channels, but format does not.

---

## Long-form post

**Platform options:** Substack, the brand's blog or newsletter, LinkedIn Article, or any owned long-form channel. The platform doesn't change the structure; it changes the metadata block at the end.

**Length:** 800–1,400 words. Sweet spot: ~1,000.

**Structure:**
- **Title** — sentence case unless brand voice specifies otherwise. Specific, not clever. Should signal the argument.
- **Subtitle** — one line that frames the stakes or sharpens the title. Optional but recommended for newsletter platforms.
- **Cold open** — 2–3 sentences. No setup. Start mid-pattern. The reader is paying attention or they're gone.
- **The diagnosis** — 1–2 paragraphs naming the pattern. This is the spine.
- **The evidence** — 2–4 paragraphs of pattern + proof. Specific examples, named vendors, observed dynamics.
- **The reframe** — 1–2 paragraphs that flip how the reader should think about this.
- **The implication** — 1–2 paragraphs that point at what should change.
- **The close** — one sharp line. Not a summary. Often the cleanest single sentence of the post.

**Voice register:** As configured for the engagement.

**Don't:**
- Use H2/H3 section headers unless the post is over 1,200 words and the structure genuinely needs them
- End with a "subscribe to read more" pitch — the platform handles the CTA
- Open with "I've been thinking about..." or similar throat-clearing

## LinkedIn carousel

**Length:** 7–10 slides. Default: 8.

**Structure per slide:**
- Slide 1 (hook): Bold pattern callout or contrarian claim. 8–14 words max. This is the swipe-or-scroll decision.
- Slide 2 (the setup): Name the dysfunction in one sentence. Add one line of context.
- Slides 3–6 (the argument): One claim per slide. One supporting line. White space.
- Slide 7 (the reframe): The "here's what's actually happening" slide.
- Slide 8 (the takeaway): One actionable line + a "follow / subscribe" prompt.

**Per-slide copy budget:**
- Headline: 4–10 words
- Body: 1–2 short sentences (under 25 words total)
- Total slide word count: under 40

**Visual direction notes:** For each slide, provide a one-line visual brief in italics:
*Visual: [layout type, key visual element, color cue if relevant]*

**Don't:**
- Use the same sentence structure on every slide
- Reuse the long-form post's opening line verbatim
- Pack three claims onto one slide

## Short-form social pack

Produce three LinkedIn standalone posts AND three X / Twitter posts. Each takes a different angle on the core argument — they are NOT the same post resized.

### LinkedIn standalone posts (3 variants)

**Length:** 150–250 words each.

**Variants to produce:**
1. **The pattern callout** — name the dysfunction. One example. One line of advice.
2. **The contrarian take** — push against a common piece of conventional wisdom from the argument.
3. **The practitioner story** — short anecdote that illustrates the pattern.

**Format rules:**
- First two lines must work as the preview before the "see more" cut
- Use line breaks generously (whitespace is the formatting)
- One CTA at the end — "Full post: [hero asset link]" or "Subscribe for more: [link]"
- No hashtag spam. Maximum two hashtags, only if genuinely useful for discovery.

### X / Twitter posts (3 variants)

**Length:** Single post under 280 chars, OR a 3-post thread.

**Variants to produce:**
1. **One-line diagnosis** — single post. The sharpest line from the long-form draft.
2. **Three-post mini-thread** — pattern → example → reframe.
3. **The screenshot bait** — a quotable claim formatted to be screenshotted and shared, ending with link.

**Format rules:**
- No "🧵" indicators on threads
- No "1/" numbering unless it's a long thread (over 4 posts)
- Link to the hero asset in the final/only post

### Channel exclusions

If the buyer doesn't publish on X / Twitter, mark X variants as out of scope and rebalance the short-form pack into additional LinkedIn variants or other configured channels (Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon — apply LinkedIn-like format rules with platform-appropriate length).

## Email nurture copy

**Structure:**
- **Subject line set** — 3 variants
  - One that names the problem
  - One that names the audience
  - One that's a curiosity hook (question format)
  - Each under 50 chars
- **Preview text set** — 3 variants, paired with subject lines (or independent)
  - Under 90 chars each
  - Don't repeat the subject line
- **Email body** — ~250 words
  - Greeting line (no "Hi [name]" unless brand voice specifies; direct address by topic instead)
  - Open with the pattern callout (1–2 sentences)
  - One paragraph of context / evidence
  - One paragraph that pitches the hero asset (long-form post or gated asset)
  - One CTA — single link, button-friendly text
  - Sign-off as specified in voice guide

**Voice register:** Configured voice softened slightly toward direct address. Less rhetorical than long-form. More conversational.

**Don't:**
- Pack two CTAs into one email
- Open with "I hope this email finds you well"
- Use emoji headers (unless brand voice specifies)
- Include "P.S." unless it's load-bearing

## Gated asset teaser

**Components:**

### 1. Landing page summary (150 words)

Three paragraphs:
- **What it is** — one paragraph naming the asset and the problem it solves
- **What's inside** — one paragraph that previews the substance (without giving it away)
- **Who it's for** — one paragraph that names the reader role and the use case

**Voice register:** Neutral business prose with one or two voice moves. This is the most "marketing" of the assets — but it should still not read as committee-written.

### 2. Three-bullet value prop block

Three bullets, each one short sentence, that promise specific outcomes:
- One outcome-focused bullet
- One credibility bullet (research basis, customer evidence, named provenance)
- One urgency or differentiation bullet

### 3. CTA copy

- Form-fill button: 2–4 words ("Get the playbook," "Download the report," "Send me the brief")
- Form headline: one line, action-oriented
- Form supporting line: one sentence about what they get and how

## SEO/AEO block

**Components:**

### SEO
- **Title tag** — 50–60 chars, includes primary keyword, sentence case unless brand specifies
- **Meta description** — 140–160 chars, includes primary keyword, ends with implied CTA (don't write "click here")
- **Primary keyword** — the one search term this post is optimized for
- **Secondary keywords** — 3–5 related terms
- **URL slug** — kebab-case, 3–6 words, contains primary keyword

### AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)
- **Question variant** — the same content reframed as a direct question (the kind a user types into ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Overview)
- **One-paragraph answer** — 40–80 words, written to be the kind of answer an AI engine extracts and quotes. Standalone, no pronouns referring back, contains primary keyword naturally.
- **Schema suggestion** — note whether the post should be marked up as Article, FAQPage, HowTo, or other

**Format the block as a code block at the top or bottom of the deliverable.**

## Optional add-ons (only if specified in brief)

### Long-form gated asset
- White paper, playbook, or ebook excerpt
- 2,000–5,000 words
- Follows long-form post structure scaled up
- Includes table of contents, executive summary, and reference section

### Video or podcast script
- Talking-head script for 3–7 minute video, OR
- Podcast talking points (15–30 minute episode)
- Adapts long-form argument for spoken delivery
- Includes hook, body, CTA, and outro

### Sales enablement one-pager
- 400–600 words
- Problem / solution / proof / next step structure
- Designed for reps to share with prospects in deal cycles

### Paid media headline variants
- 5–10 headline variants for ads
- Different filter rules apply — paid media voice tolerates short, punchy copy that would feel sales-y in earned channels
- Note: this asset uses a relaxed AI-tell filter; some patterns banned in earned content are acceptable in paid
