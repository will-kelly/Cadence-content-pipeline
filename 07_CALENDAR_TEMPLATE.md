# Content calendar block — format and sequencing logic

The calendar block is the last asset produced in Stage 3. It tells the buyer when each piece of the package should ship and why.

---

## Output format

Produce a markdown table:

| Day | Date | Asset | Channel | Time | Purpose |
|-----|------|-------|---------|------|---------|
| 0 | [hero publish date] | Long-form post | [hero channel] | Tue 8:00 AM ET | Hero asset — anchors the campaign |
| +1 | [+1 day] | LinkedIn standalone post #1 (pattern callout) | LinkedIn | Wed 7:30 AM ET | Drive long-form traffic, surface argument to LI audience |
| +2 | [+2 days] | X mini-thread | X / Twitter | Thu 9:00 AM ET | Quotable diagnosis, link back to hero asset |
| +3 | [+3 days] | LinkedIn carousel | LinkedIn | Fri 8:00 AM ET | Visual format — different surface area |
| +5 | [+5 days] | Email send | Newsletter list | Mon 7:00 AM ET | Drive subscriber reads, gated asset CTA |
| +7 | [+7 days] | LinkedIn standalone post #2 (contrarian take) | LinkedIn | Wed 7:30 AM ET | Second wave of LI reach, fresh angle |
| +10 | [+10 days] | X one-line diagnosis | X / Twitter | Sat 10:00 AM ET | Quotable line, easy reshare |
| +12 | [+12 days] | LinkedIn standalone post #3 (practitioner story) | LinkedIn | Tue 8:00 AM ET | Third wave, story-driven |
| +14 | [+14 days] | Gated asset push (if applicable) | Email + LinkedIn | Thu 7:30 AM ET | Convert engaged readers to lead-gen asset |

---

## Sequencing logic — explain after the table

Below the table, include a short rationale paragraph that covers:

1. **Why this order:** The hero (long-form) anchors. LinkedIn and X drive readers TO the hero in the first 72 hours. The email runs a few days later to capture readers who missed social. The follow-up LinkedIn posts spread the argument over two weeks so the campaign isn't a one-day blip.

2. **Why these days/times:** Default to Tuesday for hero asset (highest open rates for B2B newsletter content). LinkedIn posts mid-morning ET weekdays. X posts can flex more. Email sends Monday or Tuesday early morning. These are defaults — adjust if the buyer has analytics suggesting otherwise.

3. **Why the gaps:** No two LinkedIn posts on the same day. At least 48 hours between LinkedIn standalone post and LinkedIn carousel. The same argument needs surface area but not saturation.

---

## Sequencing adjustments by campaign goal

**Awareness campaigns:** Front-load LinkedIn and X in the first 5 days. Email can wait until day 7. Repeat the hero asset more aggressively in social.

**Lead-gen campaigns:** Push the gated asset earlier (day 3 or 4). Email body in week 1 already CTAs to the gated asset, not the long-form post. The long-form and social are warming the audience; the gated asset is the conversion event.

**Nurture campaigns:** Email runs first or second (within 48 hours). LinkedIn and X follow. The list is the priority audience.

**Sales enablement:** Skip the full calendar — assets are evergreen and shipped to sales as a package. Calendar block just notes "ship to sales [date]" and any rolling-distribution notes.

**Event drive:** Calendar is calculated backward from event date. Hero asset 14 days before. Reinforcement social in week before. Email 72 hours before. Final reminder 24 hours before.

---

## Calendar adjustments by hero channel

If the hero is the **long-form post:** sequence as in the default table above.

If the hero is the **gated asset:** swap long-form and gated asset positions. The long-form post becomes one of the supporting drivers, not the anchor.

If the hero is the **LinkedIn carousel:** carousel publishes first; long-form publishes 2–3 days later as the deeper version with carousel as visual entry point.

If the hero is a **video or webinar:** structure the campaign around the event date. Long-form publishes 7 days before as the warm-up; social drives registrations; email handles confirmations and reminders.

---

## What to ask the buyer

If the buyer hasn't given a hero publish date, ask: "When does the hero asset publish? I'll calculate everything from there."

If the buyer has given a date that falls on a low-engagement day (Sunday, federal holiday), flag it and suggest an alternative — but defer to them.

If the buyer wants a tighter timeline (e.g., everything in one week), compress proportionally but don't put two LinkedIn standalone posts in the same 24-hour window.

If the buyer publishes on regional or non-US time zones, adjust the default times accordingly. Tuesday 8 AM ET converts to Tuesday 1 PM UK, Tuesday 9 PM Singapore.
