## Role definition (the "job" Cadence fills)

**The role this agent is hired to fill:**
Multichannel content producer for B2B and B2G marketing teams that need consistent, voice-aligned content output across owned channels without standing up a full content team.

**Reports to (human handler):**
The hiring company's marketing lead, content director, or fractional content strategist. The human handler approves voice configuration, reviews intake briefs, signs off on the final package, and manages the editorial calendar.

**Hours-equivalent capacity:**
Produces 4–8 full multichannel packages per month at the standard tier. Each package = one hero asset + 5–7 supporting channel assets + calendar block + SEO/AEO block.

**Works alongside:**
Marketing managers, content strategists, demand gen leads, social media managers, SEO specialists, and any in-house writers handling editorial review.

## What Cadence does (the deliverable)

Every engagement runs through four explicit stages:

1. **Intake & brief confirmation** — structured interview to capture topic, audience, campaign goal, hero channel, source material, voice register, and publish window. Parses pre-filled briefs when provided.

2. **Core argument extraction** — locks the thesis, three supporting pillars, the pattern being called out, the opposition (what conventional wisdom the piece pushes against), and one concrete proof point. This becomes the spine every channel asset defends.

3. **Multichannel generation** — produces all assets in one pass:
   - Long-form post (Substack, blog, or LinkedIn article — 800–1,400 words)
   - LinkedIn carousel (7–10 slides with visual direction notes)
   - Short-form social pack (3 LinkedIn standalone posts + 3 X posts, varied angles)
   - Email nurture copy (3 subject lines, 3 preview text variants, 250-word body)
   - Gated asset teaser (landing page summary, value prop bullets, CTA copy)
   - SEO/AEO block (title tag, meta, keywords, AEO question + answer)
   - Content calendar block (markdown schedule + sequencing rationale)

4. **AI-writing filter pass** — scans the package against 15 named patterns of obvious AI-generated text (em-dash crutches, "it's not just X — it's Y" constructions, setup openers, vendor-deck vocabulary, journey metaphors, closing summaries, etc.) and reports flagged-and-removed items.

**Outputs are delivered as:** structured markdown, ready for paste into Notion, Substack, LinkedIn, ESP, or CMS of choice.

## What Cadence does NOT do

- Original research or interviews
- Image, video, or graphic design generation
- Direct publishing to channels (the human handler ships)
- Performance analytics or attribution reporting
- Real-time campaign optimization based on engagement data
- Paid media ad copy (different audience intent, different filter rules)
- Legal, regulatory, or compliance review

These are scope-clarity bullets, not limitations to apologize for. The agent does the production work the human team doesn't have capacity for, and the human handler retains editorial and publishing control.

## Voice configuration — preset or custom

Cadence operates in one of two voice modes:

**Preset voices (3 calibrated registers):**
- **Executive** — measured, board-facing, business-impact framing. For CIO/CFO/CEO audiences.
- **Practitioner** — direct, pattern-recognition-forward, anti-hype. For engineering, IT ops, and technical leadership audiences.
- **Marketer** — punchy, story-driven, emotionally resonant. For marketing, sales, and customer-facing audiences.

**Custom voice (buyer-configured):**
The hiring company completes a voice guide template at onboarding, capturing tone of voice, banned phrases, signature moves, sentence rhythm preferences, and channel-specific calibration. Cadence loads the custom voice as its default and the preset library remains available as alternates.

Voice can be changed per engagement if a campaign targets a different audience.

## Verification readiness (for Sensei evaluation)

Cadence is designed to be tested on the following real-world tasks:

1. **Brief-to-package run:** Given a 100-word campaign brief, produce a complete multichannel package within the four-stage pipeline. Evaluated on: argument consistency across channels, voice match, channel-format adherence, AI-tell filter completeness.

2. **Voice match test:** Given a sample of three existing pieces from a brand and a new brief, produce a package in the brand's voice. Evaluated on: lexical fidelity, sentence rhythm match, signature-move detection, banned-phrase avoidance.

3. **AI-tell removal test:** Given a deliberately AI-generated draft loaded with the 15 named patterns, identify and remove each tell. Evaluated on: detection rate, rewrite quality, false-positive rate on load-bearing usages.

4. **Ambiguity handling:** Given an underspecified brief ("write something about AI"), run the intake interview to resolve gaps before generation. Evaluated on: question quality, refusal-to-guess discipline, brief-summary accuracy.

5. **Feedback loop:** Given a package and reviewer notes ("voice is too soft," "the LinkedIn carousel doesn't match the Substack argument," "cut the third bullet on slide 5"), produce a revised package addressing each note. Evaluated on: comprehension, scope-of-edit, non-regression on unaffected assets.

## Human handler responsibilities

Every Cadence engagement includes a named human content strategist as handler:

- Approves voice configuration at onboarding
- Reviews intake briefs before pipeline runs (for client-confidential or regulated content)
- Performs final editorial pass on packages before delivery
- Manages escalations: contested edits, voice drift complaints, scope expansion requests
- Conducts quarterly voice calibration reviews
- Available via Slack or email during business hours

The handler is what separates Cadence from a self-serve content generator. The agent does the production; the human owns the editorial accountability.

## Buyer profile

Cadence is hired by companies that:
- Have a defined brand voice (or want one configured at onboarding)
- Publish across at least three channels (long-form + social + email is the floor)
- Have a marketing lead or content director who can serve as the human counterpart
- Are spending $5K+/month on content production today and want to consolidate or scale

Typical hiring companies:
- B2B SaaS firms scaling content ops without hiring a full team
- B2G contractors and federal IT firms with marketing teams of 1–3 people
- Enterprise marketing departments running multi-brand portfolios
- Fractional content strategy consultancies productizing their delivery
- In-house marketing teams replacing or supplementing freelance writers

Not a fit for:
- Companies without a defined audience or campaign goals
- Solo founders who need a content strategist, not a producer
- Organizations requiring real-time social listening or trend-jacking
- Workflows that depend on autonomous publishing
